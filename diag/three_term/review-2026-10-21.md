## Recommendation

**Keep the three-term formulation as the physical core, and build on PR121. Treat labeling, nonlinear convergence, and differentiation as three connected parts of the solver.**

After reviewing PR121 at commit `0b7535a`, including the new [labeling decision comment](https://github.com/hongkelu/vmex/pull/121#issuecomment-6332835753), and checking the VMEC2000/NESTOR and DESC implementations, my recommended direction is:

1. **Use C near \(10^{-2}\) as the provisional candidate for the next controlled experiments.** It has encouraging results, but it has not met the original accuracy requirement.
2. **Keep D as a serious alternative, and test a correctly derived VMEC modal weighting.** Current D reproduces part of VMEC's condensation condition, not its complete labeling algorithm.
3. **If neither has a weight interval that provides stable derivatives and acceptable physical accuracy, move to an independent spectral gauge constraint.** This is my preferred next structural experiment.
4. **Use a fresh scalar displacement chart if the constrained spectral gauge cannot control labels without restricting the physical shapes too much.**
5. **Make every public value, constraint, and derivative refer to one finalized, certified equilibrium.** This remains an outstanding issue in the current code.
6. **Provide exact implicit differentiation of the chosen equilibrium's stationarity equations as the correctness reference.** Keep Gauss–Newton sensitivity as a qualified approximation where it meets the error budget.

So, to answer your earlier "on top of D or another method?" question: **build the implementation on PR121's three-term infrastructure. C is currently the leading penalty candidate; D provides a useful basis for a more explicit spectral gauge if penalties cannot meet both robustness and accuracy.**

The campaign measurements below are reported in the PR. I verified the relevant code and diagnostic implementations, but did not independently rerun the GPU campaigns.

Two interpretations need correction before selecting a default.

**First, \(R_{00}\) is a diagnostic, not a uniqueness test.** Different values can arise from relabeling or different geometry. Matching \(R_{00}\) does not establish matching surfaces, matching labels, or matching stationary points. Compare the actual surfaces and physical quantities.

**Second, C's iota change is larger than the comment's quoted \(2\times10^{-4}\) suggests.** The tabulated NL and C-\(10^{-2}\) ranges have midpoints separated by about \(7\times10^{-4}\). The \(2\times10^{-4}\) number is approximately the spread between C's starts. This strengthens the need to distinguish start variation from weight-induced physical changes.

I would therefore retain "C near \(10^{-2}\)" as a **hypothesis to qualify**, rather than close P1.

## 2. Why labeling is a structural problem

For a surface \(\mathbf X(\theta,\phi)\), a small change of poloidal label gives \(\delta\mathbf X=\mathbf X_\theta\,\delta\alpha(\theta,\phi)\), which is tangential: \(\mathbf n\cdot\delta\mathbf X=0\).

In a continuous representation, it changes the labels without changing the physical surface. With truncated Fourier series, the relabeled surface generally requires additional harmonics. Projection back into the retained modes introduces a small physical change. The result is a set of nearly redundant directions that can be weakly controlled by the physical residual (Henneberg, Helander, Drevlak, arXiv:2105.00768).

That explains why the following can occur together:

- Very little change in the loss.
- Large movement in boundary coefficients.
- Significant changes in the computed sensitivity.
- Worse physical residuals when the labeling penalty is strengthened.

For the current penalty methods, the inner problem is effectively

\[
E_w(b,c)=\tfrac12\|r_{\mathrm{phys}}(b,c)\|^2+\tfrac12 w^2\|g(b)\|^2,
\]

At finite resolution, changing \(w\) can change the minimizing physical surface. Consequently, **smooth finite differences after increasing \(w\) establish numerical improvement, but do not establish physical accuracy.**

A physical quantity can have a unique derivative even if its coordinate representation is redundant. One can differentiate on a reduced space that removes the redundancy. What fails is applying an ordinary inverse to a coordinate system whose redundant directions have not been handled consistently. A robust end product should have an explicit, testable rule for controlling those directions.

## 3. What VMEC2000 + NESTOR actually does

### 3.1 VMEC uses spectral condensation with a specific constraint-force construction

\(P_m=m(m-1)\); \(G=(PR-R_{\mathrm{con},0})R_\theta+(PZ-Z_{\mathrm{con},0})Z_\theta\) (fixaray.f L192-197, funct3d.f L740-752). With the reference and scaling held fixed, the force construction is consistent with the derivative of a weighted squared condensation condition (forces.f L393-405, tomnsp_mod.f L461-488). It is not purely a mesh preconditioner.

**Implication for VMEX:** D is a sensible VMEC-inspired candidate. Adding its condition as equally weighted rows to the three-term interface fit does not reproduce the complete VMEC algorithm.

### 3.2 VMEC's reference decays; it does not track the latest accepted boundary

VMEC's reference is multiplied by 0.9 in the active free-boundary iteration; it is never reset to the latest accepted boundary (funct3d.f L548-612). Useful idea: use the initial reference to reach a canonical final labeling problem, then remove its influence. A persistent penalty relative to the previous outer optimization step makes the equilibrium depend on optimization history; updating the reference only at accepted steps or `stop_gradient` does not establish physical invariance.

### 3.3 Copying VMEC's per-mode factor directly into residual rows would be wrong

\(\operatorname{faccon}(m)=-\operatorname{signgs}/(4[m(m+1)]^2)\), \(1\le m\le \mathrm{mpol}-2\) (fixaray.f L249-251). The least-squares row multiplier is \(a_m=\sqrt{|\operatorname{faccon}(m)|}=1/(2m(m+1))\); multiplying residuals by faccon itself squares the weighting. `tcon` is geometry- and resolution-dependent (bcovar.f L891-914) and cannot be copied into VMEX's normalized residual.

**Recommended experiment:** a separately named D variant with the derived relative modal factors, documented normalization, and a global amplitude. Measure which weak directions it controls. The factors reduce the relative strength at high m, so improvement of the observed high-mode softness is not guaranteed.

### 3.4 VMEC condenses its free boundary; VMEX's fixed-boundary interior solve cannot do that for the outer fit

VMEX prescribes a trial boundary to a fixed-boundary interior problem (freeboundary_vc.py L407-474). Its internal condensation cannot choose the outer boundary coefficients.

### 3.5 NESTOR consumes the parameterization

NESTOR has no relabeling solve; the lesson comes from VMEC's coupled geometry solver. VMEC/NESTOR can support a tangential field jump (sheet current): a converged NESTOR result is not automatically the reference for the three-term zero-sheet-current problem.

### 3.6 Spectral condensation does not guarantee spacing ratio ≤ 10

Ellipse \(R=R_0+a\cos\theta, Z=b\sin\theta\): condensation vanishes, yet the spacing ratio is \(\max(a,b)/\min(a,b)\). Equal arc and condensation optimize different properties. Keep the spacing ratio as a diagnostic, but also measure quadrature accuracy, minimum surface Jacobian and spectral convergence.

## 4. What DESC and SPEC contribute

- **DESC:** keeps Fourier R,Z boundary coefficients; `FixLambdaGauge` removes only the flux-surface-average λ freedom, not geometric relabelling. Useful patterns: mode continuation and trust-region least squares for free boundary. Recent DESC work acknowledges remaining degeneracy (arXiv:2602.09957).
- **SPEC:** separates force balance from a tangential spectral-condensation condition, with the number of equations matched to the geometric unknowns (arXiv:1211.3072). Transferable principle: define the physical equations and the coordinate-selection equations explicitly, and derive the solver and derivatives from that combined definition.

### Decision table

| Outcome | Interpretation | Design choice |
|---|---|---|
| C has a stable weight interval and physical differences within the validated error budget | Simple canonical penalty suffices | Qualify one fixed C rule across all six cases |
| Properly weighted D meets accuracy and robustness more reliably | Spectral labeling suits the representation | Select D with documented normalization |
| Both smooth only after materially degrading accuracy | Labeling and shape compete at this resolution | Resolution enrichment, then a separate gauge constraint |
| Weak directions mostly tangential | Coordinate redundancy dominant | Independent spectral gauge or reduced chart |
| Weak directions have substantial normal motion or physical response | Physical sensitivity or under-resolution | Resolution, continuation, branch analysis |
| Residual/Jacobian checks fail | Fit receives an inaccurate local model | Repair evaluation/derivative consistency first |
| Distinct physical equilibria survive accurate independent solves | Genuine branch dependence | Supported branch continuation, reported |

### Preferred structural fallback: constrained spectral gauge

\(\min_b E_{\mathrm{phys}}(b,c)\) s.t. \(g_D(b)=0\); KKT: \(\nabla_b E_{\mathrm{phys}}+G_D^\top\lambda=0,\ g_D=0\). Removes the penalty-amplitude tradeoff, not finite-resolution shape bias. Must:
1. derive the independent gauge modes with the actual symmetry conventions;
2. verify they control the intended tangential directions;
3. avoid dependent rows (some real Fourier components of the odd condensation condition vanish under stellarator symmetry);
4. define a phase branch and handle degeneracy (current \(\operatorname{Im}c_1/|c_1|\) row is undefined at \(c_1=0\) and vanishes for both signs of real \(c_1\); freeboundary_vc.py L165-178);
5. check physical accuracy and resolution convergence after imposing the gauge.

Do not make every sampled equal-arc row a hard constraint.

### Next fallback: scalar displacement chart

\(\mathbf X=\mathbf X_{\mathrm{ref}}+h\,\mathbf v_{\mathrm{ref}}\), fresh implementation (no TT2 port), conditional on the spectral-gauge results; must handle reference geometry, Fourier projection errors, transversality loss, and chart changes.

## 6. Fix convergence and evaluation consistency before the next long campaigns

### 6.1 Return one certified equilibrium per design point

`_linearize` can polish the boundary and overwrite values after `fun(x)` returned (freeboundary_vc.py L1006-1042, L1079-1093). Introduce one finalization function producing a stable record: design and problem-definition ids; boundary and interior state; physical and labeling residuals; stationarity and convergence diagnostics; objective and constraint values; derivative data when requested. Every public callback, acceptance, checkpoint and report uses that record. Derivatives may add data, never change the equilibrium. Repeat physical acceptance checks on the final state (the optional physical residual check currently runs before the tight final interior solve, L960-995).

### 6.2 Make the residual and Jacobian consistent inside the boundary fit

Fit `fun` can use a loose interior solve while `jac` tightens the state (L754-780). First replay the difficult QH and QI snapshots with fully tight residual and Jacobian evaluation. If that fixes stalling or derivative noise, add controlled inexact solves afterwards.

### 6.3 Make P10 a solve target

`boundary_xtol` should drive refinement until reached, or produce a classified failure. Diagnostics: interior force convergence; scaled stationarity; undamped GN correction; predicted normal boundary displacement \(\delta X_n=(\mathbf X_b\,\delta b)\cdot\mathbf n\) (RMS and max over a dense grid, normalized by minor radius); physical residual quality; output and sensitivity stability under tighter tolerances. The ESS-scaled GN norm is not a physical distance bound; calibrate against tighter solutions.

### 6.4 Diagnose QH's stalled fit before changing algorithms

Current code can convert repeated fresh-Jacobian "stalled" outcomes into `converged=True` (L780-790). At the saved point:
1. residual directional derivatives along the proposed step and weak singular vectors;
2. predicted vs actual cost reduction over decreasing step lengths;
3. normal vs tangential motion;
4. selected exact-curvature actions;
5. log trust radius, damping, interior accuracy, rejection reasons.

Then: noise → repair evaluation; poor scaling → right preconditioning; accurate J but poor GN model → globalization/curvature; oversized outer step → continuation; branch difficulty → document. Prefer preconditioning that leaves the converged problem unchanged (reweighting residuals changes the answer).

## 7. The autodiff framework should differentiate the declared equilibrium problem

### 7.1 Exact sensitivity for the current penalty formulation

\(F=J_b^\top r=0\); \(F_b=J_b^\top J_b+\sum r_i\partial^2_{bb}r_i\); \(F_c=J_b^\top r_c+\sum r_i\partial^2_{bc}r_i\); \(F_b^\top\lambda=Q_b^\top\), \(dQ/dc=Q_c-\lambda^\top F_c\). Both curvature terms matter. Include inner equilibrium response, normalization, area weights, coil field, plasma maps, labeling, bootstrap closure. For constrained D, the full KKT system.

### 7.2 Keep GN as a measured approximation

Order: certify primal; matrix-free exact stationarity actions as reference; compare exact, GN and re-solved differences at identical settings; keep GN where it passes. The exactdir diagnostic is FD + GMRES, not production exact AD. Analytic tests first:
- \(r=(b-c,\sqrt{0.1}(b^2-1))\): at zero, exact \(db/dc=1.25\), GN 1.
- \(r=(b-c,0.5(bc-1))\): exact 1.25 from the mixed term.

### 7.3 Validate the full VMEX + ESSOS chain

Separately: coil shape coefficients, coil currents, PHIEDGE, plasma current profile and total current, pressure and bootstrap inputs. Loss and every constraint; start, middle, late snapshots at nonzero offsets; FD step sweep with a stable window; absolute tolerance near zero; JVP/VJP duality; field derivative includes direct coil change and moving evaluation points. Target ≤1e-2 relative, 1e-3 for well-resolved directions.
